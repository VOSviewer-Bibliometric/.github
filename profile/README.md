## VOSviewer Highlights

- **From Export To Map:** VOSviewer bibliometric map reads Web of Science, Scopus, or similar files from a search you performed, then lays out items.
- **Co-Citation And Bibliographic Coupling:** VOSviewer co-citation shows how cited works sit together in the set you downloaded with permission.
- **Terms From Titles And Abstracts:** VOSviewer term map builds a landscape of words in records you already exported.
- **Science Map For A Review You Write:** VOSviewer science map is a figure for a field overview, not a sociogram of classmates.
- **Not A Full Citation Genealogy:** Paper-by-paper lineages over time are CitNetExplorer’s job. This seat is the landscape.

---

## What Is VOSviewer

VOSviewer is a Windows tool from CWTS for visualizing bibliometric networks. A reviewer who already ran a systematic search opens VOSviewer bibliometric map instead of staring at 4,000 rows. VOSviewer co-citation is one network type. VOSviewer term map is another. VOSviewer science map is the labeled landscape you screenshot for a paper you write.

It is not UCINET. Nodes are publications, authors, or terms from a bibliographic database you are allowed to query, not survey respondents. It is not Cytoscape. There is no fold-change column from a wet lab. Publish or Perish answers “what are my metrics?”; VOSviewer answers “what does this corpus look like as a map?”

Use exports from searches you ran on accounts you hold, or files a librarian delivered for a review you were asked to write. Respect database terms. Do not dump a colleague’s private EndNote library into a public figure if you lack permission.

## Starting with VOSviewer

Prerequisites: A PC you administer and a bibliographic export from a search you are allowed to run.

1. **Prepare:** Run the query on the account you hold and save the export in a format VOSviewer bibliometric map accepts.
2. **Configure:** Choose co-citation, coupling, or VOSviewer term map based on the question in the review you write.
3. **Test:** Load a small year slice and confirm labels look like the field you searched.
4. **Refine:** Set thresholds so VOSviewer science map is readable, then name clusters in your notes.
5. **Apply:** Export the figure for the manuscript you own, citing the database and date.
6. **Maintain:** Keep the export with the map, and rerun when the search is updated.

## Best Uses

- **Scoping A Literature You Were Asked To Review:** VOSviewer science map of that export.
- **Author Or Journal Landscapes From A Search You Ran:** VOSviewer bibliometric map.
- **Term Clusters For A Grant You Write:** VOSviewer term map of abstracts you exported.
- **Co-Citation Of Works In Your Corpus:** VOSviewer co-citation on files you are allowed to use.
- **Not A Classroom Sociogram:** Use SocNetV.
- **Not An H-Index Desk:** Use Publish or Perish for author metrics.
- **Not A Paper Genealogy Over Time:** Use CitNetExplorer.
- **One Query Per Map:** Do not silently merge two unrelated searches.
- **Database Date In The Caption:** VOSviewer science map should say when you exported.
- **Thesaurus For Terms You Already Standardized:** Clean names before the term map.
- **Author Names Unified First:** VOSviewer bibliometric map is noisy if one person is three strings.
- **Journal Overlay Only After The Base Map:** Color later; layout first.
- **Threshold Until Labels Breathe:** A packed VOSviewer term map is not a figure.
- **Save The Map File With The Export:** VOSviewer science map should travel with the raw records.
- **Co-Authorship Separate From Co-Citation:** Do not mix those two VOSviewer co-citation-style questions in one untitled file.
- **Grant Figure Caption:** Name the query, index, and date.
- **Drop Conference Noise If The Review Excludes It:** Filter the export before the map.
